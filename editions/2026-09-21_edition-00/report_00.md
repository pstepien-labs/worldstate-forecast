## Executive summary

ASSESSMENT [M] The most important change in the balance of power in the quarter is the transition of the US and Israeli war with Iran (since 28.02.2026) from a crisis phase to a chronic state. The Islamabad memorandum (17.06.2026) collapsed in July and expired in August (CRS, IX 2026) [H], Hormuz is effectively closed, and on 11.09.2026 Saudi Arabia shut down the East–West pipeline — the main bypass route (Al Jazeera, CNBC) [H]. The IEA estimates a fall in world oil supply in 2026 of 5.7 mb/d y/y (IEA, 11.09.2026) [H]; structurally, exporters outside the Gulf (USA, Russia) gain, as does China, over which a dispersed USA has less leverage.

FACT The USA is acting in three theatres at once: since approx. 13.08.2026 there has been no US aircraft carrier in the western Pacific (AP, Stars and Stripes) [H], in Europe the number of brigades was reduced from 4 to 3 (Pentagon, 20.05.2026) [H], and on 28.07.2026 a six-month review of the force posture in Europe began (ORF) [M]. ASSESSMENT [M] The sanctions offensive against buyers of Iranian oil ("Economic D-Day", 19–24.08.2026) and the détente with China are internally contradictory — this contradiction lands on the table during Xi Jinping's visit to Washington from 24.09.2026.

FACT Talks on Ukraine resumed after six months: on 05.09 US envoys talked with Putin, on 06.09 with Zelensky, without a breakthrough (Al Jazeera; House of Commons Library) [H]; a trilateral meeting in the UAE was accepted "in principle" for October, without a date [M]. ASSESSMENT [M] The front is in a stalemate with a local Ukrainian initiative (ISW, 12.09.2026), and the Russian budget deficit after eight months (2.5% of GDP vs an annual plan of 1.6%; RF MinFin, 09.09.2026) and reports of a demand for 800 thousand additional soldiers show the rising cost of continuing the war.

FACT TTF rose to EUR 78–82/MWh in mid-September (ICE via GMK Center; ADVFN) [H] vs approx. EUR 32 a year earlier (Commerzbank, 30.09.2025) [M]; EU gas storage is 68.5% full vs 80.6% a year ago (15.09) [H]. The ECB raised the deposit rate to 2.50% (10.09.2026) [H]. ASSESSMENT Europe enters the heating season with the weakest buffer since 2022, with record imports of Russian LNG from Yamal in I–IV 2026 (Kpler/EUobserver; Urgewald/The Independent) [H].

FACT A Russian cruise missile fell in Poland on 29.07.2026 (Euronews) [H]; the North Atlantic Council condemned violations of Polish and Romanian airspace on 12.08.2026 (NATO) [H]; on 15.09.2026 NATO fighters for the first time shot down a drone over Lithuania (AP/NPR) [H]. ASSESSMENT [M] Russia's tolerance of risk on the eastern flank is rising exactly when the availability of US forces for Europe is falling.

FACT The suspension of the broader Chinese rare earth export controls expires on 10.11.2026 (S&P Global) [H]; exports of dysprosium, terbium and yttrium are 41–49% of pre-restriction volumes (BMI, V 2026) [M]; an arms package for Taiwan worth USD 14 billion is on hold (Washington Post, 23.05.2026) [H]. ASSESSMENT China is buying time and obtaining concessions on Taiwan without equivalent concessions of its own.

FACT Hungary changed government (Tisza: 138/199 seats, 12.04.2026; PISM) [H], Armenia confirmed its pro-Western course (07.06.2026; AP) [H], Venezuela is under US tutelage after the capture of Maduro (03.01.2026) [H], and in Mali the Russian security model collapsed after the JNIM offensive (25.04.2026) [H]. ASSESSMENT [M] Russia is losing the periphery (EU, Caucasus, Sahel, Caribbean), but gaining at the centre — where US attention and resources decide.

Nearest date: 24.09.2026 — Xi Jinping's state visit to Washington (Bloomberg; Reuters) [H], in which sanctions on buyers of Iranian oil, rare earths (deadline 10.11) and Taiwan lie in one package. For Poland: the current state combines high energy and military risk with the lowest availability of US reserves since 2022 — energy and defence must be treated as one area of risk management.

Two interpretations: (1) talks on Ukraine: the US delegation — talks "significant", left "encouraged"; the Kremlin (Ushakov) — "constructive"; Kyiv Post — Moscow used the envoys to pass on harder demands (08.09) [M]. **Mechanism (assessment).** The Gulf crisis is the node from which effects spread to the other regions: it takes US carriers away from the Pacific (Taiwan, Korea), brigades and attention away from Europe (the flank), raises Russian and American revenue from fuel exports, and deepens the EU's dependence on Russian LNG. The Black Sea and the Baltic are the second node: the war over shipping hits Russian exports (CPC, Novorossiysk), and Russia responds by testing NATO's boundaries. On the periphery (Caucasus, Sahel, Caribbean) Russia is losing, which does not reduce its capability in Europe over a quarterly horizon.

For Poland: the highest risk concerns airspace (drones, missiles) and Baltic infrastructure; both threats are growing under conditions of a smaller US presence.

**Mechanism (assessment).** Two chokepoints on the route from the Gulf are closed or restricted at the same time; the crisis is described as the largest disruption of energy supply since the 1970s (The Guardian via Wikipedia) [M]. Overland bypasses (E-W, ADCOP, Kirkuk–Ceyhan) have a combined approx. 9 mb/d vs approx. 20 mb/d through Hormuz (Al Jazeera; IISS via Wikipedia) [H], and the largest of them stopped working on 11.09. Europe and Asia therefore compete for cargoes from the Atlantic basin, which raises TTF and draws in Russian LNG. At the same time the Black Sea and the Baltic are the chokepoints through which Russian exports flow — and there the pressure of Ukraine and the EU is strongest.

A year ago (IX 2025): Hormuz was open (approx. 88–130 transits per day), and the Red Sea route operated at approx. 49% of pre-crisis capacity (Carra Globe) [M].

For Poland: part of the crude for Polish refineries comes from Saudi Arabia; it has not been established by which route deliveries flow after the shutdown of the E-W pipeline [L] — see J. Naftoport, Baltic Pipe and the LNG terminals gain importance as an alternative to the Gulf routes.

## F. Economic and technological rivalry

**Tariffs.** FACT On 20.02.2026 the US Supreme Court ruled (6:3) that the IEEPA does not provide a basis for imposing tariffs (Levy Institute; Holland & Knight) [H]. On the same day a 10% tariff was introduced under Section 122, in force 24.02–24.07 (Skadden) [H]; on 07.05 the Court of International Trade annulled it with respect to three plaintiffs (Skadden) [M]. From 24.07 they were replaced by new Section 301 tariffs ("forced labour"): 10% or 12.5% for approx. 60 economies, China at the 12.5% threshold plus existing Section 301 and 232 tariffs (customs services GingerControl and TariffsTool) [M]; the EU has a 15% ceiling [M]. ASSESSMENT [M] The legal basis of US tariffs is weakening, but the burden on China remains high — the effective rate fell, according to S. Evenett, from 36.8% to approx. 21.2% (Levy Institute).

**Sanctions and counter-sanctions.** FACT 19.08 "Economic D-Day", 24.08 "Operation Economic Outcast": secondary sanctions, 60 entities in the UAE, Hong Kong, China, Singapore and Switzerland (Al Jazeera; CNBC) [H]; earlier the Hengli refinery (IV), 4 Hong Kong firms (V) and 6 shipping lines (VIII) (Al Jazeera) [M]. PRC MFA: "firm opposition" and announcement of measures (25.08; Washington Post) [H]. EU: 20th package (23.04) and 21st package (23.07) — 218 listings, including 94 Russian banks; price cap frozen [H]. Two interpretations: the USA claimed that the EU had joined the sanctions on Iran; Brussels only endorsed them (press reports via Wikipedia) [M].

**Export controls, subsidies, investment restrictions.** FACT USA: H200 and MI325X to China "case-by-case" from 15.01 [H]. China: rare earths (see D), 10 US companies on the control list (VI) [M], ban on sulphuric acid exports [M]. Subsidies: Pentagon — USD 400 million in MP Materials, USD 1.6 billion for USA Rare Earth [M]; EU — SAFE EUR 150 billion [H]; NATO — over USD 50 billion of orders [H]. No new investment restrictions identified in the period (J).

**Currencies and payments.** FACT The IRGC collected Hormuz transit fees in yuan (Lloyd's List via Wikipedia) [M]. Bessent: whoever launders Iran's money "will be removed from the dollar system" (CBS, 24.08) — a fact about a statement [H]; the Atlantic Council warns of trade shifting to non-dollar channels (via Wikipedia) [M]. The EU's 21st package hits banks and crypto-asset providers, including a bank in Kyrgyzstan linked to SPFS [H]. The BRICS New Delhi declaration (12.09) adopted unanimously, condemns unilateral military action without naming states (Business Today; Reuters) [H]; no new payment mechanisms found in the available summaries [L].

**Standards.** FACT AI: the "sale with a fee" model (25% for H200) replaced a ban [H]. Drones: NATO "Drone Edge" — sources give different amounts (USD 14 billion for unmanned systems vs over USD 40 billion for counter-drone systems over 5 years; Defence Turkey; analysis by E. Saka) [L]. Energy: record electric car sales in 37 countries amid expensive fuel (money.pl, 03.06) [M]. Telecommunications — no data from the period.

ASSESSMENT Overall assessment [M]: rivalry has shifted from tariffs to control of nodes — raw materials (China), the dollar and finance (USA), sea lanes (Iran and the Houthis) and technology (USA, partly relaxed). The USA is weakening legally on tariffs, gaining as an energy exporter and taking risks in finance, because secondary sanctions on China are a test of the dollar's strength. China has the strongest single lever with a specific date (10.11), and the EU is reactive in trade but financially more active than a year ago (SAFE, a EUR 90 billion loan for Ukraine).

**Mechanism (assessment).** Secondary sanctions on buyers of Iranian oil link the Middle East theatre with the US–China relationship; the extension of the suspension of rare earth controls depends on China's response, and on that depend the costs and timelines of arms production in Europe. Every US–China financial escalation therefore passes to Europe by two routes: through energy and through raw materials for defence.

A year ago (IX 2025): IEEPA tariffs were in force; the average US tariff on China was 57% before the Busan truce; H200 was subject to a presumption of denial of export [H].

For Poland: exports to the USA are subject to the 15% ceiling (EU); the risk of secondary sanctions for Polish companies trading with entities linked to Iran or the Russian SPFS has increased.
