**Notation conventions**

- **FACT** — an event with a date (dd.mm.yyyy), a publisher and a record identifier in `02_facts/` (e.g. G1-001). The full record holds the URL, the source rating in the Admiralty code (A–F / 1–6), the perspective (W Western · A actor's side · T third party) and the PIR link. For key facts the source rating is given in brackets, e.g. [B/2]. Records LT-01…LT-12 are stage 03 records dated 17.11.2026 before the information cutoff (`03_analysis.md` §1.7); A-01…A-06, B-01…B-08 and C-01…C-09 were fetched by the forecasting lenses in stage 04, R-01 by the red team (`05_red_team.md` §7); they have the full record format. Records dated after 16.11.2026 are marked **(after the state date)**; they are facts, but not state values.
- Event status: **DONE** carried out and confirmed · **DECL** declaration or announcement · **DISP** disputed. A government statement (Washington, Moscow, Beijing, Tehran, Brussels, Warsaw) is a fact about a statement, not about an event.
- **ASSESSMENT** — inference by the author of the report, always with analytic confidence (low / medium / high). Confidence is the quality of the evidence, not a probability.
- **FORECAST** — numerical probabilities appear only in section H and in the annex. The official forecast is **AGG_RT** (the mean of lenses A, B, C after accepted red-team adjustments). Verbal text follows methodology §10: almost ruled out 1–5% · very unlikely 5–20% · unlikely 20–45% · roughly even chances 45–55% · likely 55–80% · very likely 80–95% · almost certain 95–99%.
- Disputed events: two interpretations, marked (1) and (2), not decided without evidence.
- Facts carried over from edition 02 after confirmation in stage 02 carry the note "confirmed unchanged, dd.mm.yyyy".
- Outlets on the no-republish list (Russian state media, sanctioned outlets) are paraphrased as "according to state agency X" or "a statement by X" and never quoted.
- Comparisons with the crowd and prediction markets are not part of this report (methodology §6); they are in the separate file `07_annex_benchmarks.md`. The one exception is the aggregate score "BSS vs the crowd" in section 0, an official measure of methodology §1 computed only on questions already resolved (`01_scores.md`); it carries no crowd value for any open question.

## Executive summary

**Iran: the war returned to land at low intensity, and Washington closed its own diplomatic channel.** FACT: for the first time since 01.09 the US struck Iranian territory — Qeshm on 02.11, described by US officials as "defensive strikes", and on 06.11, when the President spoke of "minor strikes" on an anti-aircraft site (Al Jazeera, Times of Israel, Meduza; G1-005) [B/2]. An unattributed strike hit a radar site at Sirik on 12–13.11 (IRNA via RFE/RL; G1-006) [B/2]. Iran answered against a host state: drones toward US sites in Bahrain on 13.11 and 15.11, intercepted according to CENTCOM and Bahrain's military (RFE/RL, The National; G1-006, G1-007) [B/2]. Three US carriers were in the Arabian Sea on 16.11 (USNI; G1-011) [B/2]; the blockade continues — a cargo ship was disabled on 07.11, and CENTCOM says it kept Iran from exporting more than 25 bn USD of crude (Al-Monitor citing Reuters; G1-009) [B/2]. After a short opening (a Vance letter and Doha contacts, 28–31.10; G4-001) the President wrote on 12.11 "We don't need a deal with Iran" and took the Vice President off the file (Dawn, Al Jazeera; G4-007) [B/1]. Tehran conditions any talks on "seven conditions" (G4-003) while President Pezeshkian says the Leader "has ordered an end" to "neither war nor peace" (Tasnim; G4-004) [B/1]. ASSESSMENT (confidence: medium): coercion without a defined exit; both sides calibrate their strikes to stay below a full campaign.

**The oil shock reversed; diesel and gas did not.** FACT: ICE Brent front month rose to a wire-confirmed settlement of 116.72 USD/bbl on 23.10 (stage 01) and a vendor close of 117.64 on 30.10, then fell about 16% in the week to 06.11 and closed at 92.44 on 16.11 (Investing.com, CNBC citing Reuters; G2-001, G2-002) [B/2]; Dated Brent fell from 135.51 (02.10) to 92.14 (11.11) (EIA via FRED; G2-003) [A/1]. The IEA now sees a 4Q26 surplus of about 1.7 mb/d, with Gulf crude exports at 82% of pre-war in October (IEA OMR, 13.11; G2-004) [A/2] — while PortWatch's AIS count in Hormuz stayed at 0–8 transits a day (G1-001) [A/2]. Diesel stays the binding shortage: about 180 USD/bbl in Europe, +77% on pre-war (IEA; G2-004). TTF peaked at 103.94 EUR/MWh on 30.10, the highest since 2022, and closed at 84.05 on 16.11 (Investing.com; G2-011) [C/2]; EU storage peaked at only 74.96% (27.10) and was 70.55% on 14.11, Germany 57.30% (AGSI+ via Global Energy Flow; G2-012) [B/2]. Orlen terminated one of its two QatarEnergy LNG contracts on 10.11, citing the continuing force majeure (Orlen current report via PAP Biznes; G2-014) [A/2].

**Russia–Ukraine: process and escalation at the same time; Washington spent its sanctions leverage.** FACT: on 16.10 Executive Order 14436 set and at once waived all duties and most sanctions of the Graham Act (H.R. 5334), without an expiry date (whitehouse.gov; G3-001) [A/1]; an OFAC licence for Russian diesel runs to 07.04.2027 (NBC; G2-007) [B/2]. At the Kremlin on 12.11 Russia proposed a new "indivisible security" architecture with the US recalling its 2021 draft treaties (kremlin.ru via Lenta.ru; G4-019) [A/2]; on 16.11 the Kremlin aide Ushakov said Moscow had not agreed to the European seat the EU had just claimed for its "E5" team (Interfax via Meduza; G4-021, G4-022) [B/1]. In parallel the energy ceasefire announced by Trump on 11.10 did not take effect (G1-036, G4-014), Russia struck Ukraine's grid in November (Ukrenergo, Ukrainska Pravda; G1-037) [B/2], and Ukraine hit the Kerch bridge on 16.11 (Moscow Times, Kyiv Post; G1-039) [B/3]. Russia's finances absorb the war: a January–October deficit of 2.3% of GDP (Ministry of Finance via Interfax; G3-017) [B/1] and a 2027–2029 budget passed in first reading 372–0 (Kommersant; G3-016) [B/1].

**NATO's flank: the Black Sea campaign reached a member's territorial waters; Poland's airspace stayed quiet.** FACT: at least 14 attacks on merchant ships off Romania and Bulgaria since early October (Reuters via gCaptain; AP; G1-020) [B/2]; on 14.11 three ships were hit near the Sulina canal, inside Romania's territorial waters, and the BETUL sank on 16.11 (Reuters, Euronews; G1-021) [B/2]. The Polish defence minister says the Pentagon "greenlit" a permanent US presence in Poland, with a White House decision in early December (Defense News; G1-030) [B/2]; candidate sites are Poznań and Wrocław (Defence24; G1-031) [B/3].

**Washington lost Congress; the energy shock moved into central banks.** FACT: the Democrats won the House and the Senate on 03.11 (AP via PBS, NPR; G4-033) [A/1]; Vice President Vance: "The reason we lost is the war" (G4-002); Stephen Miller replaced Susie Wiles as chief of staff on 09.11 (NPR, CBS; G4-034) [B/1]. Federal funding runs only to 11.12 (CRS; G3-056) [A/2]. The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% on 28.10 with two dissents for a hike, and Governor Waller leans to a December hike (federalreserve.gov, CNBC; G3-036, G3-038) [A/1]; the Bank of England raised its rate to 4.00% (G3-042) [A/1]; the ECB held at 2.50% after debating a hike, with euro-area flash HICP at 4.0% for October (ecb.europa.eu, Eurostat; G3-040, G3-041) [A/1]. The US 10-year yield stood at 5.15% on 13.11 (FRED; G3-039) [A/1].

**USA–China: the truce was formalised to January; the friction moved to chips and Taiwan's airspace.** FACT: both sides renewed every suspension before the 10.11 date — MOFCOM/GAC Announcement No. 58 continued the suspension of the October-2025 export controls to 10.01.2027 (G2-027) [A/2]; USTR suspended the maritime s.301 actions and extended 178 exclusions to 09.01.2027 (Federal Register; G3-008, G3-009) [A/1]; China's Tariff Commission kept the 24% tariff suspended for two months (G3-010) [A/1]; BIS extended the Affiliates-rule suspension to 10.01.2027 (G2-032) [A/1]. The US-specific gallium, germanium and antimony ban suspension still ends on 27.11 with no extension found by 17.11 (LT-06) [B/3]. New friction: a BIS rule on AI chips for five third countries and remote access (16.11; G2-031) [B/2] and US sanctions on two Chinese firms over Cuba (G3-013) [B/2]. Taiwan's defence ministry published six releases on PLA "Joint Combat Readiness Patrols" between 09.10 and 12.11, with a 2026 high of 49 aircraft on 02.11 (Taiwan MND; G1-041) [A/2], while no US carrier was in the Western Pacific (USNI; G1-044) [B/2]. After the state date, the first Trump–Xi–Putin meeting was scheduled for Shenzhen on 19.11 (White House, PRC MFA, Kremlin; G4-025, LT-05) [A/2].

**Poland: the cost of capital became a domestic-politics question.** FACT: S&P revised Poland's outlook to negative on 06.11 and lowered the local-currency rating from A to A-, citing polarisation and presidential vetoes (Notes from Poland, Parkiet; G3-046) [B/2]. A Constitutional Tribunal crisis has run since 30.10; on 16.11 the NBP stopped the Tribunal's payments (Euronews, Polsat News, TVN24; G4-049) [B/1]. Fuel prices are capped by decree (diesel maximum 7.52 PLN/l on 14–16.11; G2-018) [B/2], and gas storage was 99.2% full in early November (PAP/GIE; G2-013) [B/2]. A terrorist shooting in Kraków on 07.11 killed six people (France 24, Notes from Poland; G4-050) [B/2].

**For Poland.** ASSESSMENT (confidence: medium): in six weeks the escalation signals and the de-escalation signals both grew. Escalation came in kind — US strikes on Iranian land, Iranian drones at Bahrain, Houthi strikes on Riyadh and the East-West pipeline, ship attacks inside Romanian waters, a GRU plot in Estonia — but none crossed the thresholds that would turn a protracted crisis into a coupled one: Gulf exports recovered and Brent fell. De-escalation came in process — the Abu Dhabi trilateral, the formalised US–China truce, the Red Sea traffic returning — but not in settlements. For Warsaw the decisive dates are now concentrated in early December: the US decision on a permanent presence (about 01–04.12), the MPC (01–02.12), the 2027 budget vote (04.12), the US funding deadline (11.12) and the ECB (17.12). The base scenario "Protracted crisis without resolution" remains the most likely; its distribution and the 83 forecasts are in section H and the annex.

## A. Strategic goals of the great powers

### USA

FACT: three files, three different uses of leverage. *Iran:* limited strikes on Iranian territory (Qeshm 02.11 and 06.11; G1-005) [B/2], a blockade enforced by force (G1-009) [B/2] and three carriers in the Arabian Sea (G1-011) [B/2]; diplomacy opened briefly — a Vance letter to SNSC secretary Rezaei and Iranian talks with Qatari mediators in Doha on 28–31.10 (The National, Dawn; G4-001) [B/2] — and closed on 12.11: "We don't need a deal with Iran. Those days have long since passed" (Truth Social via Dawn and Al Jazeera; G4-007) [B/1]. Reuters (14.11, via Ukrinform and RFE/RL) reports that the President told advisers he may hold off strikes if Iran and the Houthis refrain from new provocations (G1-010) [B/3]. Rubio (10.11): Iran "has to eat eventually" (Kurdistan24; G3-033) [B/2]. *Russia:* sanctions relief traded for process and energy — the diesel licence of 09.10 (G2-007), the Graham Act waiver of 16.10 with Rubio's 60-day impact review (whitehouse.gov, NBC; G3-001, G3-002) [A/1], Witkoff's meeting with Putin in St Petersburg on 21.10 (OSW, RFE/RL; G4-015) [B/2], the Abu Dhabi trilateral (G4-018) and no OFAC Russia designation between 22.10 and 13.11 (ofac.treasury.gov; G3-032) [A/1]. *China:* the truce was formalised to January on the US side (G3-008, G3-009, G2-032) [A/1], while Bessent and Greer pressed Beijing on 06.11 for "concrete steps" on farm purchases and rare earths (The Standard citing Reuters; G2-028) [B/2], the State Department sanctioned two Chinese firms over Cuba on 05.11 (G3-013) [B/2] and BIS issued an AI-chip rule on 16.11 (G2-031) [B/2]. *Europe:* a Pentagon "green light" for a permanent presence in Poland according to the Polish minister, the Pentagon readout silent (Defense News; G1-030) [B/2]; reported cut options in Europe down to about 5,000, mostly in Germany (Bloomberg via search summaries; G1-032) [C/3]. *Domestic constraint:* the midterm loss (G4-033) [A/1], a FY2026 deficit of 1.992 trn USD (Treasury MTS; G3-055) [A/2] and funding only to 11.12 (G3-056) [A/2].

ASSESSMENT (confidence: medium): the edition 02 pattern "leverage accumulated, use deferred" split in two. Toward Iran Washington now *uses* force in calibrated doses and pairs it with economic strangulation and a public refusal to negotiate — coercion without a stated exit. Toward Russia it *spent* leverage (the diesel licence, the Graham Act waiver) to buy process (the trilateral, a prisoner release) and energy relief. The midterm defeat, which the Vice President attributed to the war, did not soften the Iran line; it removed the main internal advocate of a deal from the file. With a Democratic Congress from 03.01.2027, the administration has about six weeks of unified control.

### China

FACT: Beijing renewed every suspended measure before its deadline and kept all of them legally reversible. MOFCOM/GAC Announcement No. 58 (09.11) continued the suspension of the six October-2025 export-control announcements to 10.01.2027 (Sina Finance, SMM; G2-027) [A/2]; the April-2025 licensing of seven rare earths was never suspended (G2-027). The State Council Tariff Commission kept the 24% additional tariff on US goods suspended for two more months and the 10% in place (The Paper, Guangming Daily; G3-010) [A/1]. The first meeting of the US–China Trade Council (13.11) discussed the "30 bn for 30 bn" lists without publishing lists or rates (MOFCOM via Xinhua; G3-011) [A/2]. The PBoC raised the FX reserve requirement ratio from 4% to 6% from 15.11, the first rise since 2021, to slow the yuan's rise (gov.cn, Caixin; G3-015) [A/1]. Beijing "firmly opposes" the US Cuba sanctions on its firms (Al Arabiya, SCMP; G3-014) [B/2]. Around Taiwan the PLA raised its patrol tempo (G1-041) [A/2] while Xi, on 12.11, restated that "complete reunification … will certainly be realised" without mentioning force (Al Jazeera, DW; G1-043) [B/2]. China hosts the first Trump–Xi–Putin meeting on 19.11, framed by its MFA as "strategic stability in major-power relations" (Dawn citing Mao Ning; G4-025) [A/2]; on 02.11 Xi said the Gulf should not become a battleground for great-power rivalry (Al-Monitor citing Reuters; G4-070) [B/2].

ASSESSMENT (confidence: medium): "suspension, not repeal" is Beijing's operating rule — every concession expires on a date chosen to coincide with the next negotiation (10.01.2027), which keeps the raw-materials lever loaded while Washington holds the chip lever. Hosting the trilateral lets China present itself as a convenor between Washington and Moscow without paying for either war.

ASSESSMENT (confidence: medium): Moscow gives Washington process and keeps substance.
