## 1.6 Mirror test

**A. Places where the analysis may assume that another actor weighs costs and benefits the way the West does:**

1. **Iran — economic strangulation as pressure to concede.** Rubio's "has to eat eventually" (G3-033) and the ACH both treat blocked exports as pressure toward H1. Tehran's answer — strikes on host states, mining claims, Ghalibaf's "dual-use" rule (LT-03) — is consistent with a regime that values demonstrating it can raise others' costs more than restoring its own revenue.
2. **USA — the midterms as a brake on war.** We read Vance's "the reason we lost is the war" (G4-002) as a domestic constraint. The President's response was the opposite ("no deal needed"; G4-007); with Congress changing hands in January, the administration may judge the next six weeks as a window to act, not to wait.
3. **Russia — the trilateral as a step toward peace.** We count Abu Dhabi (G4-018) as process. Moscow's added demand for a European security treaty with the US (G4-019) suggests it values the format as a way to separate Washington from Europe; "process" may be the goal, not the path.
4. **Russia — Black Sea attacks as tests of NATO.** As in edition 02, the targets are ships trading with Ukrainian and Moldovan Danube ports (G1-021); hits in Romanian waters may be accepted collateral rather than a probe of Article 4.
5. **China — suspensions as goodwill.** We treat Beijing's renewals (G2-027, G3-010) as a preference for stability. Their structure — short, reversible, timed to negotiations — may reflect a view that the lever is worth more held than used or given up; Chinese firms raising buffer stocks from 30 to 90 days (G2-027) shows the domestic side plans for reinstatement.
6. **Houthis — restraint at sea as a cost calculation.** KA8 assumes the Houthis spare shipping to avoid US strikes. They may instead spare it because the Red Sea transit regime (HOCC requests; G1-050) gives them a revenue or legitimacy channel they value — which would make the pause more durable than a pure deterrence reading suggests.
7. **Saudi Arabia — coalition-building as compensation for US absence.** Riyadh's Syrian and Turkish partners (G1-019) may reflect a choice of autonomy; Trump's account that Riyadh asked him to "let it be the way it is now for a while" (G4-007) is one-sided and may not reflect Saudi preferences.
8. **Poland — the US base as a pure security good.** Washington's timing and site choice may follow transactional or political logic (competing offers from Romania and Lithuania; G1-033).
