**ASSESSMENT (confidence: medium).** Beijing goes into the summit with its raw-materials leverage intact and without visible concessions; the restriction of magnet exports to the USA just before the summit looks like a signal rather than a coincidence (one monthly observation — low confidence as to intent).

## 1.6 Mirror test

**A. Places where the analysis may assume that another actor weighs costs and benefits the way the West does:**

1. **Iran — the cost of a closed strait.** Assumption KA1/H1 assumes that lost exports and the blockade will push Tehran to concessions. For the IRGC a closed strait is a source of income (fees of approx. 1–2 mn USD per ship, G1-059, single source) and deterrence; losing this lever may be judged a greater cost than sanctions. The growth of demands (G4-003) is consistent with this.
2. **Iran — the split as a tactic.** The analysis treats the contradictory signals of the MFA and the IRGC as a division of power centres (KA2). It may just as well be a division of roles ("good cop, bad cop") within a single decision — in which case the 7-day offer has substance.
3. **Russia — the deficit as a constraint.** KA7 and the assessment of "rising cost of continuation" assume that a deficit of 2.5–3% of GDP constrains the war. The Kremlin shows that it raises taxes (VAT 22%) and maintains spending; the binding constraint may be the labour market or inflation, not the deficit.
4. **Russia — the energy ceasefire.** The Western framing (power grid, heating) is not the Kremlin's framing: for Moscow "energy" means exports and tanker security (G4-021). Questions and indicators on a ceasefire must have a criterion of confirmation by both sides, not a declaration by one side.
5. **China — the economy over leverage.** KA3 assumes that the weak PRC economy pushes Beijing towards a truce. Before the Fifth Plenum (26–29.10) on party discipline, Xi may value an image of toughness towards the USA more than short-term trade.
6. **USA — elections as a brake.** KA1 assumes that fuel prices before 03.11 restrain Washington. The administration may judge that a quick operation against Iran mobilises voters — Trump's signal "either an agreement, or it will all end very quickly" (G4-005) does not rule out this logic.
7. **The Houthis and Saudi Arabia.** The analysis assumes that the Houthis act as an extension of Iran (the Yemen condition in Tehran's demands). The Houthis also wage their own war for Yemen (offensive against the PLC, G1-021) and may escalate independently of the US–Iran talks.
