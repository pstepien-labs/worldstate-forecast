**ASSESSMENT (confidence: medium).** Washington keeps every lever cocked and defers its use to dates after 03.11 (Iran strikes, posture review) — with one exception it cannot defer without a formal act: H.R. 5334, whose 30-day term ends on 18.10. The pattern of edition 01 ("leverage accumulated, use deferred") holds; what is new is that the administration is now asking allies to pay for its constraints (European diesel stocks, Polish and Lithuanian offers to finance bases — G1-037, G1-040).

**China.** Beijing secured the extension of the Kuala Lumpur arrangement to 10.01.2027 (suspension of the US 24% reciprocal tariff, the affiliates rule, s.301 maritime fees, and "related Chinese export controls"; MOFCOM 28.09, G3-010) without naming rare earths and without amending the legal end date (10.11.2026) of the rare-earth suspension (G2-041). Its teapot refiners lost Iranian crude and pay up to +18 USD/bbl for Iraqi grades (G2-035).
**ASSESSMENT (confidence: medium).** China converted the summit into a time extension that keeps its raw-materials lever legally intact (the rare-earth text still says 10.11) — the same "leverage kept" pattern as Washington's. The cost of the Iran blockade now falls partly on Chinese refiners, which gives Beijing an interest in a Hormuz settlement but no visible role in brokering it.

**ASSESSMENT (confidence: medium).** Moscow prices the war upward rather than seeking an exit: it raises defence spending by about a quarter, finances it with new taxes and borrowing, and blocks the trilateral format while keeping a bilateral channel with Washington that includes economic offers (Lukoil assets; NYT via Meduza, G4-020). **Hormuz contradiction — two interpretations:** (1) most Gulf exports move through the strait AIS-dark, under US escort or Iranian clearance — one escorted tanker switching off AIS was reported (G1-006), and both JPMorgan and Goldman say much of the flow is untracked (G2-005); (2) the flow estimates overstate traffic — Iran's own adviser speaks of about 10 ships/day (G1-005), Iran loaded no crude at all (G2-012), and LNG transits remain about 75% below pre-war (G2-022).
**ASSESSMENT (confidence: low).** Both series can be true only if a large dark flow exists; no dataset measures it. **Mechanism.** Two regimes now govern the strait: the US blockade (for Iranian ships) and the IRGC clearance rule (for everyone else). ## 1.6 Mirror test

**A. Places where the analysis may assume that another actor weighs costs and benefits the way the West does:**

1. **Iran — zero exports as pressure to deal.** The ACH treats zero crude loadings (G2-012) as pressure toward H1. For the IRGC, a regime in which Iran controls others' exports through clearance may be judged more valuable than restored own exports under US terms; the route rule (G1-004) is consistent with this. Revenue loss may harden, not soften, the position.
2. **Iran — the impeachment motion as a sign of weakness.** We read the Majlis dispute (G4-009) as fragmentation. It may equally be a coordinated signal to Washington that any MFA concession lacks domestic cover — raising Iran's price rather than lowering its capacity.
3. **USA — elections as a brake, then as a release.** KA1 assumes strikes are deferred because of 03.11. After 03.11 the same logic may run the other way — or the administration may judge that a pre-election operation mobilises voters. The explicit "after the elections" framing (G1-010) is itself a statement and may be signalling.
4. **Russia — the deficit and the 1% of GDP cost.** We treat Putin's admission (G1-051) and the doubled deficit (G3-016) as constraints. The Kremlin's response — more defence spending, new taxes, FX purchases from high oil revenue — shows that it budgets the cost rather than reacting to it; the binding constraint may be inflation and labour, not money (repeated from ed.01, now with evidence).
5. **Russia — the Black Sea campaign as "escalation against NATO".** From Moscow's side the target is cargo to Ukraine (G1-033); hits in RO/BG EEZs may be accepted collateral, not a probe of NATO. Treating them as tests of Art. 4 projects a Western reading of intent.
6. **China — truce as preference for stability.** KA3 assumes Beijing values the truce. China's hardening toward the EU at the same time (G3-051, G3-052) shows it separates fronts; it may accept friction with Europe precisely because it has stabilised the US front.
7. **Saudi Arabia — dependence on the US.** We read the Mecca Alliance (G1-020) as compensation for US abstention. Riyadh may also be choosing autonomy — a regional coalition gives it freedom to escalate in Yemen without US constraints.
8. **Poland — the base as a pure security good.** The analysis treats the US base as a PIR-2 gain. Washington may value it as a transaction and a lever in Polish domestic politics (G4-049, DISP); the base's timing may follow political rather than military logic.
