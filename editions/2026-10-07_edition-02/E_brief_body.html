Bottom line · Assessment · confidence: medium

In two weeks no fact that decides between the scenarios has changed: Hormuz is still closed in shipping data, there is no agreement on Iran or Ukraine, and the US–China truce was extended without settling rare earths. What changed is the weight of the crisis signals: Saudi deaths, a wave of tanker strikes, ships sunk in NATO members’ waters, and a US military build-up timed for after the 03.11 midterms. The base case, a protracted crisis without resolution, remains the most likely.

What changed this edition

Iran: the war moved to sea

FACTNo US strike on Iranian land since 01.09. The US blockade had redirected its 130th vessel by 05.10. On 05.10 the IRGC said ships may use only an IRGC-cleared northern route. At least 10 tankers were struck in and near Hormuz between 01.10 and 06.10, and no attacker has been named.

The Saudi–Houthi war is its own front

FACTHouthi strikes killed 3 and injured 36 at Riyadh and Abha airports (06–07.10), and 3 more were killed on 08.10. The Saudi–Pakistani–Turkish “Mecca Alliance” was activated on 05.10. The US stays out “for now”.

Lethal strikes in NATO waters

FACTThree merchant ships were sunk or set on fire in the Romanian and Bulgarian EEZs on 05–06.10, and 2 crew died. No government has attributed the attacks. Russia warned NATO over Kaliningrad, including a nuclear warning.

Diesel is the binding shortage

FACTBrent futures 100.20 USD (07.10), but spot Dated Brent 113.96 (29.09). European diesel hit a record 2.24 EUR/l, prompting a G7 stock release of up to 100 mn bbl (02.10). After the state date, the US licensed imports of Russian diesel (GL 135).

US–China: time bought, texts unchanged

FACTThe truce was extended to 10.01.2027, but the legal end date of China’s rare-earth suspension is still 10.11.2026. H.R. 5334 obliges the President to act on Russia-linked tariffs by about 18.10, unless he uses the waiver.

Russia budgets for a long war

FACTThe 2027 draft budget puts defence at 17.15 trn RUB (old plan 13.57). Putin’s terms are unchanged (01.10), and Moscow has “no current plans” for the US-proposed three-way talks with Ukraine (07.10).

Reading guide

How to read this brief

Three kinds of statement, always labelled

FACTAn event with a date, a publisher and a record ID in the edition’s fact files, e.g. G1-007. A government statement is a fact about a statement, not about the event.

ASSESSMENTOur inference, always with a confidence level (low / medium / high). Confidence describes the quality of the evidence. It is not a probability.

FORECASTA probability for a question with a fixed deadline and an objective resolution rule. The official value (AGG_RT) is the average of three independent analytic lenses after red-team review. It is frozen in a public, append-only registry before outcomes are known, and scored afterwards.

DISPUTEDTwo credible interpretations are given, one line each. We do not choose between them without evidence.

AFTER STATE DATEA fact dated after 07.10.2026. It was used by the forecasters, but it is not part of the state picture.

Source ratings

Ratings such as [B/2] use the Admiralty code: source reliability from A (reliable) to F, and information credibility from 1 (confirmed) to 6. Every key event is checked against a Western source, a source from the actor’s side and a third-party source; gaps are recorded in the full report.

Where this comes from

The Gulf and Iran

Strait of Hormuz · blockade · diplomacy
Bottom line · Assessment · confidence: medium

The land ceasefire holds, but the sea is not quiet: the US blockades Iranian exports and Iran restricts everyone else’s. Washington itself has moved the risk of escalation to the weeks after 03.11. In Tehran the negotiating mandate is contested, which weakens any commitment the foreign ministry can make.

Region 5 of 6

Europe, energy and global markets

Oil · diesel · gas · rates · sanctions
Bottom line · Assessment · confidence: medium

The energy shock has changed shape. Crude futures are near 100 USD while the physical market is much tighter. Governments have shifted from relying on markets to releasing stockpiles and cutting taxes, which moves the cost onto budgets. Central banks are tightening while growth weakens, a stagflation set-up, and long-term yields at 24-year highs are themselves now tightening conditions.

About this brief

Method, gaps and provenance

How the forecasts are made

Each two-weekly edition runs nine stages. Facts are collected with a three-perspective rule (a Western source, a source from the actor’s side, and a third-party source). Three forecasting lenses then work blind to each other: great-power game, outside view, and domestic and economic constraints. A red team reviews them, and the aggregated forecasts are frozen in an append-only registry before any comparison with markets or crowds. Questions are scored with the Brier score against simple baselines.

Accuracy so far

Of 27 resolved questions, 17 have been scored; 10 await verification. The official forecast scored a Brier of 0.117, against 0.302 for the status-quo baseline (skill score 0.613). By vector, the weakest is military (0.216, n = 5). With n = 17, these are early, indicative numbers.

What we do not know

  • The real oil flow through Hormuz: no dataset measures traffic with trackers switched off.
  • Who in Tehran holds the negotiating mandate.
  • Whether the White House sent Congress the H.R. 5334 notice or waiver. This is not publicly observable.
  • The size and timing of the US posture change in Europe; the base figures rest on one Polish official.
  • Israel as an independent actor: no indicator covers an Israeli strike on Iran.
  • Some primary data were read only through aggregators (EU gas storage, ICE settlements).
