Bottom line · Assessment · confidence: medium

In six weeks the signals of escalation and of de-escalation both grew. Escalation came in kind: US strikes on Iranian land, Iranian drones at Bahrain, Houthi strikes on Riyadh and the East–West pipeline, ship attacks inside Romanian waters, a Russian sabotage plot in Estonia. None crossed the thresholds that would turn a protracted crisis into a coupled one: Gulf exports recovered and Brent fell. De-escalation came in process (the Abu Dhabi three-way talks, a formalised US–China truce, Red Sea traffic returning) but not in settlements. The base case, a protracted crisis without resolution, remains the most likely.

Reading guide

How to read this brief

Three kinds of statement, always labelled

FACTAn event with a date, a publisher and a record ID in the edition’s fact files, e.g. G1-005. A government statement is a fact about a statement, not about the event.

ASSESSMENTOur inference, always with a confidence level (low / medium / high). Confidence describes the quality of the evidence. It is not a probability.

FORECASTA probability for a question with a fixed deadline and an objective resolution rule. The official value (AGG_RT) is the average of three independent analytic lenses after red-team review. It is frozen in a public, append-only registry before outcomes are known, and scored afterwards.

DISPUTEDTwo credible interpretations are given, one line each. We do not choose between them without evidence.

AFTER STATE DATEA fact dated after 16.11.2026, before the forecasts' information cutoff. Used by the forecasters, not part of the state picture.

Source ratings

Ratings such as [B/2] use the Admiralty code: source reliability from A (reliable) to F, and information credibility from 1 (confirmed) to 6. Every key event is checked against a Western source, a source from the actor’s side and a third-party source; gaps are recorded in the full report.

Where this comes from

This brief adds no facts, assessments or forecasts of its own. Every number is taken from the edition 03 report (07_report.md) or the registry (registry/forecasts.csv). Record IDs point to the full report, which is the primary document. Nothing here is investment advice.

The Gulf and Iran

Strikes on land · blockade · Hormuz
Bottom line · Assessment · confidence: medium

The US and Iran now trade strikes on land, and neither has an exit. Washington strikes in small doses, strangles Iran's exports and refuses to negotiate. Tehran answers by raising the cost to third parties, Gulf host states and shipping, rather than by conceding, and no authority there is visibly able to sign and deliver a deal. Both sides calibrate their strikes to stay below a full campaign.

Red Sea, Saudi Arabia and the oil route

Houthis · East–West pipeline · Bab el-Mandeb · Brent
Bottom line · Assessment · confidence: medium

Russia, Ukraine and NATO’s eastern flank

Talks · grid strikes · Black Sea · Baltic
Bottom line · Assessment · confidence: medium

Moscow gives Washington process and keeps the substance. It accepted the three-way format it had refused, but widened the agenda to a European security treaty negotiated with the US without Europe, which turns the Ukraine talks into a negotiation about NATO. Europe, energy and global markets

Gas · diesel · rates · sanctions
Bottom line · Assessment · confidence: medium

Europe's energy shock moved from oil into gas and central banks. Europe enters the winter with gas storage about 15 points below normal and Germany near 57%; the price risk sits in TTF, the physical risk in a cold winter with continued Qatari force majeure. EU measures that need unanimity (sectoral sanctions, the 2027 Ukraine loan, the long-term budget) are slowed by the same four governments.

What could prove us wrong

About this brief

Method, gaps and provenance

How the forecasts are made

Each two-weekly edition runs nine stages. Facts are collected with a three-perspective rule (a Western source, a source from the actor’s side, and a third-party source). Three forecasting lenses then work blind to each other: great-power game, outside view, and domestic and economic constraints. A red team reviews them, and the aggregated forecasts are frozen in an append-only registry before any comparison with markets or crowds. Questions are scored with the Brier score against simple baselines. This edition ran unattended, and interrupted steps were continued from where they stopped.

Accuracy so far

Of 67 resolved questions, 66 have been scored with 77 forecasts; 1 awaits verification. The official forecast scored a Brier of 0.173, against 0.375 for the status-quo baseline (skill score 0.538). By domain, the weakest is energy (0.237, n = 12), the strongest financial (0.122, n = 13). Forecasts made 14 days or less before the deadline scored 0.135; longer ones 0.217.

What we do not know

  • The real traffic through Hormuz: ship-tracking rows were partly unreadable, and the escorted flow is not measured by any open dataset.
  • No public CENTCOM release on the November strikes on Iran was found.
  • The Saudi official side and the Houthi side were read only through aggregators.
  • The US posture change in Europe and the Polish base decision rest on Polish ministers and on reports via search summaries.
  • No third-party assessment of the front line in Ukraine could be read.
  • Israel as an independent actor: no indicator covers an Israeli strike on Iran.
